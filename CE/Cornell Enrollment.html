Overview

The Cornell Enrollment Mobile App is a mobile first redesign of Cornell University Student Center. It transforms a complex enrollment portal into a guided smartphone experience built for clarity and confidence. The redesign focuses on reducing confusion and uncertainty during high stakes actions such as adding, dropping, and swapping classes.

1. Empathize

Because enrollment is time sensitive and stressful, I focused on the biggest points of friction in the current system and how a mobile first experience could reduce uncertainty.

  • Mobile usability is poor. Students often check schedules and make enrollment decisions while walking to class or during busy weeks. The portal is not designed for one hand use and feels difficult on a phone.
  • Risky actions feel intimidating. Drop and swap feel high risk. Students fear losing a seat, making an irreversible mistake, or not understanding what will happen after confirming.
  • The interface creates overload. Dense tables, heavy text, and too many links make it hard to scan quickly. Students want fast answers such as whether a class is open, whether it conflicts, and whether an action succeeded.

Informal interviews and observations

  • Confidence matters as much as information. Students want to know what will happen before submitting. Confirmation is as important as navigation.
  • Students want direct access to key actions. They do not want to hunt through menus to reach schedule, search, add, drop, and swap.
  • Students want clarity on rules and consequences. Waitlist behavior, time conflicts, unit limits, and what swap actually does should be explained in plain language.

2. Define

People problem: During add and drop week, students need to make high-stakes academic decisions quickly, often while moving between buildings. The current enrollment experience is confusing and not mobile-friendly, which increases stress and the risk of mistakes.

  • Navigation complexity. Students jump between multiple pages and dense menus. The flow is hard to follow when rushed.
  • Lack of trust during risky actions. Drop and swap are not explained clearly. Students do not feel reassured that the action is safe or reversible.
  • Cluttered information display. Long tables make scanning difficult. Status such as Open, Waitlist, and Closed is not visually prominent enough to support fast decisions.

Target audience: Cornell undergraduates ages 18 to 25 who manage enrollment during busy academic periods and want fast decisions, clear feedback, and mobile convenience without needing a laptop.

Goal: Create a mobile-first enrollment experience that makes add, drop, and swap feel clear, guided, and safe by reducing clutter, simplifying navigation, and providing strong feedback and confirmations.

3. Ideate

Based on the problems above, I explored solution directions and early structural concepts that could reduce stress while respecting existing enrollment constraints.

  • Simplify layout. Replace table-heavy pages with card-based UI and highlight only the information students need to decide quickly.
  • Make actions guided. Break add, drop, and swap into structured steps: select, review, confirm, result.
  • Build trust. Add warnings before risky actions and confirmation screens that clearly state what changed with one clear next step.
  • Create a clear navigation system. Use bottom navigation for key tasks: Home, Search, Schedule, Enrollment Hub, Profile.

Mid-fidelity exploration

To validate these ideas before moving into high-fidelity design, I created mid-fidelity wireframes focused on structure, hierarchy, and task clarity. At this stage, the goal was not visual polish but interaction confidence during time-sensitive enrollment actions.

  • Step-based progression. Introduced progress indicators to help students understand where they are in add, drop, and swap flows.
  • Status visibility. Tested quick-scan color indicators for Open, Waitlist, and Closed to support faster decisions.
  • Strong confirmation states. Designed clear success feedback to reduce anxiety about whether changes were processed.
  • Schedule visualization. Explored a block-based layout to help students quickly detect time conflicts.
  • Clear action hierarchy. Evaluated primary and secondary button placement to guide students toward safe next steps.

4. Prototype

I created an interactive mobile prototype that reflects a realistic enrollment journey, from checking today’s schedule to safely confirming schedule changes.

  • Student Center Home. Today’s schedule, quick actions for add, drop, swap, plus enrollment dates and holds summary.
  • Add flow. Search and find classes, add to cart, confirm enrollment, and a success screen with View My Schedule.
  • Drop flow. Select an enrolled class, review a warning step before dropping, confirm, and see an updated schedule.
  • Swap flow. Select a class to replace, choose a new class, review the swap, confirm, and see a clear result screen.
  • Profile and academic records. Grades, unofficial transcript access, course history, and transfer credit entry points.

This redesign does not only modernize the interface. It improves how students feel while using the system by reducing uncertainty, increasing confidence during risky actions, and enabling faster decision-making in time-sensitive enrollment moments.
