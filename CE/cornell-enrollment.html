Cornell Enrollment Mobile App

Cornell’s Student Center was designed primarily for desktop workflows, yet students frequently manage enrollment on their phones between classes. This project explores how a mobile-first redesign can make enrollment faster, clearer, and less stressful.

01

1. Empathize

To understand how students interact with the enrollment system, I conducted informal interviews and walkthrough sessions with Cornell undergraduates during add/drop week.

Students were asked to walk through how they normally add a class, drop a class, and swap a class, while navigating the current Student Center interface.

Key Observations

  • Students hesitate before confirming enrollment changes
  • Swap is perceived as risky and unclear
  • Important information is buried in dense tables
  • Students double-check schedules after submitting actions
  • Mobile experience is difficult and not optimized

Research observations

Students often paused before confirming actions, re-read key screens, and checked their schedules again afterward. The issue was not only navigation complexity, but low confidence in the outcome.

Research synthesis

I grouped interview notes into themes around scanability, feedback, and confidence during risky actions. That synthesis helped define the core design direction for the rest of the project.

Key Insight
The core problem is not just usability. It is lack of confidence during high-stakes actions. Students are not just trying to complete tasks. They are trying to avoid making irreversible mistakes.
Dense tables slow down scanning on mobile. Swap feels risky because the consequence is not obvious enough. Users also double-check outcomes because the system feedback feels weak.

02

2. Define

Students need a faster and safer way to manage enrollment on mobile devices without feeling uncertain about the outcome of their actions.

The current system slows decision-making because it requires navigating multiple pages, presents information in dense tables, and provides weak feedback after actions.

Design Goals

  • Reduce cognitive load and make course information easy to scan quickly
  • Increase confidence in risky actions
  • Improve mobile navigation with fewer steps
  • Improve system feedback so actions clearly succeed or fail

Early structure

Mid-fidelity wireframes were used to test layout, navigation, and enrollment steps before moving into visual design. The goal was to make common tasks easier to find and easier to follow on mobile.

Mid-fi refinement

Course detail and drop flows were simplified around clearer hierarchy and scannable cards, while swap was redesigned as a guided sequence instead of a table-heavy utility flow.

Guided swap logic

The wireframes helped establish a step-by-step structure for swap so students could better understand what they were replacing, what fit their schedule, and what would happen next.

03

3. Ideate

After synthesizing research insights, I explored ways to restructure the enrollment experience for mobile.

The ideation process focused on reducing cognitive load, increasing confidence in risky actions, improving navigation speed, and handling edge cases such as conflicts, waitlists, and prerequisites.

Concept Direction

A key idea was shifting enrollment from a form-based system into a guided workflow. Instead of navigating tables and forms, users move through Select → Review → Confirm → Result.

Design Directions Explored

  • Card-based course layout for faster scanning
  • Step-by-step enrollment flows
  • Side-by-side swap preview
  • Visual schedule blocks for conflict detection
  • Clear status indicators: Open, Waitlist, Closed
  • Strong confirmation and success states

Enrollment is not just a usability problem. It is an emotional experience. The ideation phase focused on reducing anxiety and helping students feel confident in their decisions.

Faster entry point

The redesigned dashboard makes core actions easier to spot right away, giving students a clearer path into enrollment without first decoding a desktop-style interface.

Clearer course decisions

The add flow makes status, course details, and next steps easier to scan at a glance. Instead of forcing users to decode a dense table, the screen surfaces what matters first and supports faster decisions on mobile.

Less risky drop flow

The drop flow is designed to reduce hesitation. Course information stays readable, the action remains clear, and confirmation feels more explicit so users are not left wondering whether the change actually went through.

Stronger confirmation

Confirmation is treated as an important part of the experience, not an afterthought. The screen clearly reflects the updated schedule and reassures users that the action is complete.

Guided swap sequence

Swap is one of the most stressful actions in the existing system, so the redesign turns it into a more guided sequence. Step labels, clearer hierarchy, and stronger previews make the process easier to follow.

Better preview before commit

Before users confirm a swap, the interface gives them a clearer sense of what they are changing and why it fits. That added visibility helps reduce second-guessing during a high-stakes action.

Consistent product system

Supporting screens like profile, grades, and transcripts extend the same visual language. That consistency makes the app feel more cohesive and less like a collection of disconnected admin pages.

Final Design Features

  • Card-based UI for easy scanning

Cornell’s red was used as a primary accent to maintain branding while highlighting important actions.

5. Test
Quick usability testing was conducted with students using the prototype. Tasks included adding a class, swapping a class, and dropping a class. Students completed tasks faster, hesitated less before confirming actions, and found that swap preview, schedule blocks, and confirmation screens improved confidence and reduced the need to double-check.

Key Insight
Strong feedback and structured flows significantly reduce uncertainty.

Reflection
This project highlighted how administrative systems often prioritize functionality over user confidence. While the original system technically works, it creates unnecessary stress during an already high-pressure process.

What I Learned
UX is not just about efficiency. It is about confidence. Clear feedback is critical in high-risk interactions, and mobile-first design requires rethinking structure, not just resizing screens.

Future Improvements
Real-time waitlist updates, push notifications for seat availability, and integration with long-term academic planning tools.
