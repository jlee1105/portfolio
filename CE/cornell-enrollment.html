Cornell Enrollment

A mobile-first redesign of Student Center focused on making add, drop, and swap feel faster, safer, and easier to trust during high-stakes enrollment moments.

Mobile UX Interaction Design Prototype Student Product
Problem
Desktop-first enrollment flow on a phone
Focus
Reduce uncertainty in add, drop, and swap
Outcome
Guided flows, clearer status, stronger confirmation
Overview

What this redesign solves

This version shifts the page from class-project document energy into a cleaner product case study: fewer walls of text, stronger hierarchy, and screens that do more of the storytelling.

Students were not just navigating slowly. They were hesitating because the system felt risky.

Cornell’s Student Center was built around desktop tables and multi-step utility flows. But students often manage enrollment on their phones between classes, under time pressure, and with real consequences if they make a mistake.

The redesign focuses on three things: making information easier to scan, making high-risk actions easier to understand, and making outcomes feel obvious the moment an action is completed.

Role
UX / UI Designer
Methods
Interviews, walkthroughs, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing
Primary design principle
Help users know what will happen before they commit
01

Research and pain points

I conducted informal interviews and walkthrough sessions with Cornell undergraduates during add/drop week. The most important pattern was not just navigation friction. It was low confidence.

  • Students re-read confirmation screens because they were unsure what would happen next.
  • The swap flow felt especially risky because replacing one course could accidentally remove another.
  • Seat availability, waitlists, and time conflicts were buried inside dense tables.
  • Many students manually checked their schedule again after submitting an action because they did not fully trust the feedback.
Pain point
Dense tables slow down scanning on mobile.
Pain point
Swap feels risky because the consequence is not obvious enough.
Pain point
Users double-check outcomes because the system feedback feels weak.
02

Design direction

Instead of treating enrollment like a form-heavy admin workflow, I reframed it as a guided mobile product flow. The redesign replaces tables with cards, makes status visible earlier, and walks users step by step through add, drop, and swap.

  • Reduce cognitive load with card-based layouts and stronger hierarchy
  • Show open, waitlist, and closed status more clearly
  • Preview consequences before a high-risk action is confirmed
  • Use confirmation states that feel final and easy to trust
03

Final screens

The final design keeps Cornell red as the main accent while using a cleaner visual system to support action hierarchy. The screens emphasize speed, readability, and confidence. Key changes include stronger status indicators, clearer schedule visualization, and confirmation states that remove ambiguity.

04

What improved

In quick usability testing, students completed add, swap, and drop tasks with less hesitation than they described in the current Student Center experience. The strongest improvements came from more visible system status, better previews before committing, and clearer confirmation states after actions were complete.

Clearer
Card-based information and schedule blocks reduced the need to decode dense tables on mobile.
Safer
Swap and drop flows made consequences easier to understand before users confirmed.
Faster
Important actions became easier to reach without bouncing between multiple admin-style pages.
