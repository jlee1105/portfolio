Fan Experience

01 · Opportunity

The fan journey becomes fragmented before the puck even drops

Fans may need separate destinations for team content, schedules, venue guidance and ticket actions. I reframed the concept around one question: how might a Bruins-first product help a fan understand what matters now and take the next action quickly?

01

Competing information

The original screens gave news, schedules and game information nearly equal visual weight.

02

Changing navigation

Fans had to relearn where tickets, roster information and stats lived.

03

Disconnected tickets

Seat information, wallet access and transfer were not presented as one journey.

04

Limited context

The product needed to respond to pregame, live and postgame moments.

02 · Research approach

Evaluating the complete fan journey before redesigning it

I evaluated the original experience through a competitive audit, heuristic review and task-based cognitive walkthrough. I mapped the fan journey around five critical tasks: finding an upcoming game, locating seat information, accessing and transferring a ticket, identifying the projected goalie and comparing player leaders.

Heuristic review

I evaluated visibility of game status, navigation consistency, label clarity, error prevention in ticket actions and readability under time pressure.

Journey mapping

I organized needs across before the game, entering the arena, following the matchup and checking results afterward.

Competitive framing

I separated four product roles: team identity, league-wide information, venue operations and secure ticket management. The opportunity was connecting them without pretending to replace specialized services.

Assumption mapping

I prioritized the highest-risk areas: the Bruins-first game hub, the distinction between ticket details and the secure mobile ticket, and the placement of lineup and matchup information.

The original home treated multiple content types with similar emphasis. The current matchup and next useful action should be recognizable without scrolling. Created a contextual Game Day home with opponent, time, venue, ticket details and arrival information. The redesigned hierarchy exposed the opponent, start time, venue and ticket action within the opening view.
The monthly schedule compressed opponents, results and times into small calendar cells. Home and away matchups should be distinguishable without reading every schedule entry. Introduced All, Home and Away filters with consistent matchup cards. All, Home and Away filters created direct paths to the relevant matchup and venue information.
Seat information and ticket access were presented as if they were the same action. Separating informational details from the secure credential reduces confusion and accidental exposure. Separated View Ticket Details, Open Mobile Ticket, Add to Wallet and Transfer. Distinct labels created predictable paths for checking a seat, opening the credential, adding it to a wallet and transferring it.
Roster and statistical information required several disconnected screen patterns. Consistent role filters and Team/Players controls help fans predict where information lives. Connected roster filters, player profiles, team metrics and sortable player rankings. Position filters, lineup tabs and Team/Players controls made the projected goalie and player leaderboard discoverable.
Long all-capital content and dense statistics reduced readability. Condensed display type should communicate sports identity while readable body type handles longer content. Reserved display typography for headings and numbers while using a conventional sans-serif for articles and supporting copy. Separating display typography from body typography improved hierarchy and reduced long all-capital reading.
Evaluation result

All five critical tasks had a discoverable path through the final experience. The walkthrough produced refinements to ticket labels, selected states, transfer feedback, schedule filters and the separation between team performance and player rankings.

03 · Product strategy

Four destinations, with game day as the organizing layer

I reduced the global navigation to Home, Schedule, Tickets and More. Roster, stats and news stay accessible while the home experience prioritizes the current game and the fan’s next likely action.

05 · Game day

Prepare, follow the matchup and understand the team in one place

The game hub progresses from practical pregame details to projected lineups and matchup statistics. Tabs preserve context while fans move from logistics to hockey information.

06 · Tickets

Ticket information and secure ticket access are clearly separated

“View ticket details” opens seat, entrance and holder information without implying a QR code is visible. “Open mobile ticket” is reserved for the secure entry credential. Wallet and transfer actions remain secondary but available.

07 · Team and content

Quick answers with clear paths into deeper exploration

Roster filters support browsing by role. Player profiles combine current performance, history and draft details. Team/Players switches between team performance and leaderboards. News follows a familiar feed-to-article pattern.

08 · Design system

A Bruins identity without sacrificing product readability

Typography

Barlow Condensed gives headings and numbers a broadcast voice. Inter supports readable body text and controls.

Color

Black and neutral gray create hierarchy. Bruins gold is reserved for active states and primary actions.

Components

Reusable cards, tabs, stat rows and navigation keep 38 screens consistent.

Accessibility

Readable text, touch targets, icon labels and contrast support use in a busy arena.

10 · Reflection

The strongest improvement was not adding more features. It was making each moment tell the fan what matters now.

The final system connects game-day preparation, tickets, schedules, team information and editorial content through one consistent mobile structure. Evaluating the five critical tasks helped me simplify navigation, clarify ticket terminology and prioritize information according to the fan’s immediate context.
