Case Study

VCT Sync

A mobile experience designed for Valorant Champions Tour fans who want structured match insights without clutter or distraction.

Overview

VCT Sync is a mobile esports companion designed for Valorant Champions Tour fans who want fast, meaningful match insight without clutter. While existing platforms emphasize dense statistics and aggressive monetization, VCT Sync prioritizes clarity, momentum, and mobile scan speed.

The goal is not simply to show who won, but to help fans quickly understand what changed the match and why it mattered.

1. Empathize

This research involved semi-structured interviews with several active VCT viewers, ranging from casual followers to competitive ranked players. Rather than profiling individuals, the focus was on common patterns that surfaced in real tasks.

Participants

I spoke with three VCT fans with different viewing habits:

  • Participant A: Watches most matches live, cares about bracket implications
  • Participant B: Watches highlights and checks scores between classes
  • Participant C: Ranked player, uses stats to learn and improve

Method

Semi-structured interviews plus task walkthroughs on mobile. I asked participants to use their current apps and talk out loud while completing the same tasks.

Research approach

To understand real usage behavior rather than surface-level opinions, I structured interviews around task-based walkthroughs. Participants were asked to:

  • Find the result of a missed match
  • Identify what shifted momentum
  • Navigate from live match to bracket
  • Locate roster information

I observed hesitation points, scanning behavior, and navigation patterns.

Observed patterns

Across interviews, three consistent behaviors emerged:

  • Participants looked for score first, then immediately searched for context.
  • Users skipped dense stat tables unless they were highly engaged fans.
  • Navigation friction increased dramatically on mobile screens.

I noted how long it took to locate key information and where confusion arose.

What users kept repeating

  • "I want to know what mattered fast, not scroll through numbers"
  • "I hate when ads break the flow, it makes me leave the app"
  • "I check score first, then I need a quick explanation of how the match swung"
  • "Brackets are confusing on mobile when everything is tiny"

Research synthesis

Affinity mapping of interview notes surfaced three core themes:

  • Fans want contextual storytelling, not just final scores
  • Intrusive ads damage trust and interrupt focus
  • Dense layouts increase cognitive load during quick check-ins
Affinity map: Interview themes and behavioral patterns.

2. Define

Problem statement

When VCT fans miss a match, they want to catch up in under 60 seconds. Current esports apps make this hard because they prioritize dense stat tables and monetized layouts over a clear story of what changed and what it means for the tournament.

Design principles

  • Scan first design: Score, momentum, and bracket impact visible within one screen
  • Context over volume: Show the few stats that explain the outcome, hide the rest until requested
  • Zero interruption: No disruptive ad placements and no clutter that breaks focus
  • Spoiler control: Users choose how much is revealed when browsing

Design goal

Create a narrative-driven, mobile-first experience that surfaces what mattered most while reducing cognitive load.

Scope

VCT Sync focuses on match catch-up and tournament tracking. It does not try to replace full VOD viewing or deep analytics sites.

3. Ideate

With research themes identified, the process moved into structured ideation. Each idea was framed against research insights to ensure alignment with user needs.

How might we

  • How might we help fans understand momentum without reading a full stat table?
  • How might we make bracket impact obvious on mobile?
  • How might we keep the experience focused without disruptive monetization?

Concepts explored

  • Momentum timeline with pivotal rounds labeled
  • Match summary cards: turning point, clutch player, and key stat
  • Spoiler mode toggle for navigation
  • Bracket view with highlight of next match and stakes
  • Lightweight reactions or chat layer for shared viewing

What I prioritized and why

I prioritized momentum storytelling plus fast navigation because every participant started with score, then immediately searched for context. Community features were kept lightweight to avoid clutter and keep the core flow fast.

4. Prototype

Primary flow

  1. Open the app and land on Live or Recent matches
  2. Tap a match to see score and momentum story
  3. Swipe or tap into key moments for context
  4. Jump into bracket to see what the result changes
  5. Optional: open deeper stats only if needed

An interactive high-fidelity prototype was developed to test this flow and validate hierarchy decisions.

5. Test

While VCT Sync is a concept project, task-based walkthroughs revealed measurable improvements in clarity and navigation compared to participants’ described experiences with existing esports apps.

During follow-up walkthroughs:

  • Participants located live match results immediately without guidance
  • Users identified pivotal match moments faster when using the momentum hierarchy
  • Bracket navigation required fewer scroll adjustments after hierarchy refinements
  • Participants described the interface as “clean,” “focused,” and “less overwhelming”

Most notably, users no longer needed to switch platforms to understand match context. The streamlined hierarchy allowed them to understand both outcome and momentum within a single view.

These results validated the decision to prioritize clarity and progressive disclosure over dense statistical dashboards.

Iteration example: match hierarchy

Initial observation

In early versions, the live match screen displayed score and stats with similar visual weight. During walkthrough testing, participants immediately located the score but hesitated when identifying key momentum shifts.

Design adjustment

To address this, I:

  • Increased visual emphasis on pivotal round markers
  • Reduced competing stat density in the primary view
  • Strengthened contrast hierarchy between score and secondary data

Result

Participants were able to describe the match flow faster and reported that the layout felt more focused and less overwhelming.

Constraints and tradeoffs

  • No official live data integration. Because this was a concept project without direct API access, the design had to assume limited real-time data control. This influenced the decision to prioritize clarity and summary views over complex stat dashboards.
  • Balancing depth and simplicity. Hardcore fans want depth, while casual viewers want speed. Instead of overloading the main interface, progressive disclosure ensures deeper stats appear only when requested.
  • Spoiler sensitivity. Some users prefer immediate full results, while others avoid spoilers. The design explores spoiler-aware navigation to accommodate both behaviors.
